How Scholia intends to serve other languages

The architecture below is a design, not a running system. It is published so partners can evaluate the approach before anything is built on it.

Concept — not yet built
01
SOURCE
Tagged Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek; morphology, syntax, variants
02
MEANING
Semantic graph; referents, ambiguity, discourse
03
DECISION
Rationale and alternatives, with no hidden interpretive steps
04
LANGUAGE
Grammar, orthography, idiom, community terminology
05
REVIEW
Native-speaker and consultant validation before any release

The principle: English is an output of the graph, not the master source every other language is translated from.

What we will not say

Published deliberately, because a project like this is judged by the claims it refuses to make.

  • We will not say that AI translated the Bible. AI drafts; people with names and credentials authorize.
  • We will not promise a whole Bible in thousands of languages on a timeline, before a single pilot has a measured baseline.
  • We will not imply that a language without a complete Bible has no Scripture at all. Most have a New Testament or portions.
  • We will not describe the organizations already doing this work as slow or obsolete. The fastest path runs through them, not around them.
  • We will not show a verse as reviewed, tested or approved until a named human has done it.
